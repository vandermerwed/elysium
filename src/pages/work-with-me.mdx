I'm Daniel van der Merwe. I run [Rokkit200](https://rokkit200.co), a software agency in South Africa, and I have spent 18 years building on the .NET and Optimizely stack. Most of my working day is now spent steering AI agents rather than typing, and I have been building the operating system that makes that work. A knowledge vault my agents read and write. The conventions they work under. The small tools that keep me out of the glue work between apps.

I try things on myself before I ask a team to do them. Everything below comes from that.

## Talks

I speak to engineering teams and leadership groups about what actually changes when agents do most of the typing. The current talk is Software for one, on building personal software with AI agents, first given at DayOne in September 2026. The other is [the five levels of organisational AI maturity](https://rokkit200.co/insights/five-levels-of-organisational-ai-maturity), the ladder I use to place a team before anyone talks about tooling.

## Workshops

A half day or a full day with a team that has been experimenting with AI coding tools and wants an engineering practice out of it. We work on the repo's conventions, how review changes when an agent wrote the diff, what an agent may and may not reach, and how the team decides what to trust. We leave with changes in the repo, not a slide deck.

## Advisory

Short engagements with technology leaders working out where AI fits in their delivery organisation, or who want a second opinion on an architecture or platform decision before it gets expensive. I have worked on [regulated on-prem-to-cloud migrations](https://rokkit200.co/insights/leading-a-regulated-cloud-migration), and most of what went wrong on them was decided before the first workload moved.
